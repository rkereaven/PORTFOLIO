About me

An 18-year-old from the Philippines who likes knowing how things work.

I'm Andrew, a computer engineering student at Mapúa University. I finished senior high school in the STEM strand at San Beda University in Manila, where my group built an Arduino-based safety device for our capstone research. That project is what made me want to study engineering.

I'm also interested in joining the Institute of Computer Engineers of the Philippines (ICpEP) for its hackathons and its network.

Projects

AlertSafe

Senior high capstone · San Beda University

An Arduino-based accident detection device for a motorcycle helmet. If a rider crashes and cannot call for help, AlertSafe senses the impact, sounds an alarm, and texts the rider's GPS location to emergency contacts. Eight of us in 12-STEM 1 researched and tested it, with Ms. Laurabel S. Bacones as research adviser.

Key features

  • Impact detection with an MPU-6500 accelerometer
  • Sound alarm to alert people nearby
  • GPS-SMS message with the rider's coordinates

Built with

  • Arduino
  • MPU-6500
  • GPS-SMS
  • Sound alarm

Drop test: how often the device activated

Result and what I learned

The device activated in 33% of drops at 0.5 m and in none at 1.5 m or 3.0 m, so its impact threshold was not accurate and it could not yet detect real crash-level impacts. We concluded it has potential but needs better durability and calibration. Seeing a test fail honestly taught me why engineers measure first and claim later.

Personal journey

From San Beda to Mapúa, and learning to keep up.

Success relies less on innate talent and more on developing a proper mindset and discipline.

From my CW2 reflection paper

Before college

I finished senior high school in the STEM strand at San Beda University. My group's capstone, AlertSafe, was my first real engineering project. We built and tested it while dealing with online classes, which taught me how much a team depends on each other's effort.

The shock of a faster pace

High school gave us time between lessons to rest and prepare. At Mapúa, the term system covers a whole high school semester's worth of content in much less time, and quizzes and problem sets pile up quickly. I learned the hard way in my first days that professors give the main points and expect you to practice on your own. That taught me to be proactive.

Drum Corps and school

As a drummer in the Mapúa Drum Corps, I have weeks when rehearsals and performances compete with academics. My fix is a weekly schedule that blocks out Drum Corps time first and fits studying into the remaining slots, so schoolwork doesn't take a back seat. I also review each lesson right after class, because small reviews are easier to schedule than long study sessions.

Better study habits

Active practice works better for me than rereading. I try problems before looking at solutions, set aside fixed slots for problem sets, and plan to join a peer study group, since explaining problems out loud shows me what I don't understand. In Design Thinking I also picked procrastination as my challenge and used mind mapping to tackle it.

What's next

I want to join ICpEP for hackathons and to meet upperclassmen and professionals who can give advice. My goal this year is the dean's list, and after that a career at a high-tech company.
